## Introduction

English has **four present tenses**, and mixing them up is one of the most common sources of mistakes for learners — especially for IELTS Writing and Speaking, where picking the wrong present tense changes the meaning of a sentence (a habit vs. an action happening right now vs. something that started in the past and continues today). This lesson gives you all four present tenses side by side so you can compare their forms and uses directly. For the deepest dive into Present Simple specifically (full rules, spelling, and exercises), see [`present simple.md`](./present%20simple.md).

---

## Overview: The 12 English Verb Tenses

English verb tenses are organized along two axes: **time** (Past / Present / Future) and **aspect** (Simple / Continuous / Perfect / Perfect Continuous). Seeing all 12 together makes it easier to understand where the four present tenses fit.

| ENGLISH VERB TENSE | Past | Present | Future |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SIMPLE** | I **studied** English yesterday. | I **study** English. | I **will study** English. |
| **CONTINUOUS** | I **was studying** English. | I **am studying** English. | I **will be studying** English. |
| **PERFECT** | I **had studied** English. | I **have studied** English. | I **will have studied** English. |
| **PERFECT CONTINUOUS** | I **had been studying** English. | I **have been studying** English. | I **will have been studying** English. |

> This lesson covers the **Present** column in depth: Present Simple, Present Continuous, Present Perfect, and Present Perfect Continuous.

---

## What are the Present Tenses?

The four present tenses are used to:
- Describe habits, routines, and general facts (**Present Simple**)
- Describe actions happening right now or temporary situations (**Present Continuous**)
- Connect a past action/experience to the present moment, with no exact time given (**Present Perfect**)
- Emphasize the **duration** of an action that started in the past and continues now, or just stopped (**Present Perfect Continuous**)

### Definition
A present tense expresses an action, state, or habit viewed from the point of "now" — but each of the four present tenses frames "now" differently: as a general truth (Simple), as an in-progress moment (Continuous), as a connection to the past (Perfect), or as an ongoing duration (Perfect Continuous).

## Rules & Formation

### 1. Present Simple — habits, facts, routines

**Structure:**
| Subject | Verb |
|---------|------|
| I, You, We, They | play / work / study |
| He, She, It | play**s** / work**s** / studi**es** |

**Negative**: Subject + do/does + not + verb
**Question**: Do/Does + Subject + verb?

**Examples:**
- I **study** English every day.
- She **doesn't like** coffee.
- **Does** he **work** on Sundays?

> Full rules, spelling (-s/-es), and exercises: see [`present simple.md`](./present%20simple.md).

---

### 2. Present Continuous — happening now, temporary, future arrangements

**Structure: Subject + am/is/are + verb-ing**

| Subject | am/is/are | Verb-ing |
|---------|-----------|----------|
| I | am | studying |
| He, She, It | is | studying |
| You, We, They | are | studying |

**Negative**: Subject + am/is/are + not + verb-ing
**Question**: Am/Is/Are + Subject + verb-ing?

**Examples:**
- I **am studying** English right now.
- She **isn't working** today.
- **Are** you **coming** to the party tonight? (future arrangement)

**Key uses:**
- Action happening at the moment of speaking — "She is reading a book."
- Temporary situations — "I am living with my parents this month."
- Fixed future arrangements — "We are meeting the client tomorrow."

---

### 3. Present Perfect — past action connected to the present

**Structure: Subject + have/has + past participle**

| Subject | have/has | Past Participle |
|---------|----------|------------------|
| I, You, We, They | have | studied / been / done |
| He, She, It | has | studied / been / done |

**Negative**: Subject + have/has + not + past participle
**Question**: Have/Has + Subject + past participle?

**Examples:**
- I **have studied** English for five years. (unfinished time, continues now)
- She **has never been** to London. (life experience, no exact time)
- **Has** he **finished** his homework yet? (recent past, result matters now)

**Key uses:**
- Life experience — "I have visited Japan twice."
- An action that started in the past and continues now — "They have lived here since 2015."
- A recent action with a present result — "I have lost my keys." (I don't have them now)
- With time words: **already, yet, just, ever, never, since, for**

---

### 4. Present Perfect Continuous — emphasis on duration

**Structure: Subject + have/has + been + verb-ing**

| Subject | have/has been | Verb-ing |
|---------|----------------|----------|
| I, You, We, They | have been | studying |
| He, She, It | has been | studying |

**Negative**: Subject + have/has + not + been + verb-ing
**Question**: Have/Has + Subject + been + verb-ing?

**Examples:**
- I **have been studying** English for three hours. (still going, duration emphasized)
- She **has been working** here since 2020.
- **Has** it **been raining** all day? (and maybe it just stopped — recent result visible, like wet streets)

**Key uses:**
- Duration of an action that is still happening — "I have been waiting for an hour."
- An action that recently stopped but has a visible present result — "I'm tired because I have been running."

---

## Examples

### Present Simple vs. Present Continuous
- I **work** at a bank. (permanent fact) vs. I **am working** on a report right now. (happening now)

### Present Perfect vs. Past Simple
- I **have lived** in Cairo for 10 years. (still living there — unfinished) vs. I **lived** in Cairo for 10 years. (finished — I don't live there anymore)

### Present Perfect vs. Present Perfect Continuous
- I **have read** three books this month. (focus on the completed result / quantity) vs. I **have been reading** this book for a week. (focus on the ongoing duration)

## Common Uses

| Tense | Example | Frequency |
|-------|---------|-----------|
| Present Simple | "I study English every day." | Very common |
| Present Continuous | "I am studying right now." | Very common |
| Present Perfect | "I have studied English for five years." | Common |
| Present Perfect Continuous | "I have been studying since 6 PM." | Common |

## Study Exercises

### Exercise 1: Choose the Correct Present Tense
**Level: Beginner-Intermediate**

1. She _______ (study/is studying) English every morning.
2. Look! It _______ (rains/is raining) outside.
3. I _______ (have lived/live) in this city since 2018.
4. He _______ (has been working/works) on this project for three hours and he's exhausted.
5. They _______ (don't like/aren't liking) spicy food.

**Answers:** 1. studies  2. is raining  3. have lived  4. has been working  5. don't like

---

### Exercise 2: Present Perfect vs. Past Simple
**Level: Intermediate**

1. I _______ (visit) Paris last year.
2. She _______ (never/visit) Japan.
3. We _______ (finish) the project yesterday.
4. He _______ (already/finish) his homework.
5. _______ you ever _______ (try) sushi?

**Answers:** 1. visited  2. has never visited  3. finished  4. has already finished  5. Have ... tried

---

### Exercise 3: Multiple Choice
**Level: Intermediate**

1. I _______ English for two years.
   - [ ] A. study
   - [ ] B. am studying
   - [ ] C. have been studying

2. She _______ dinner right now.
   - [ ] A. cooks
   - [ ] B. is cooking
   - [ ] C. has cooked

3. _______ you ever been to Egypt?
   - [ ] A. Do
   - [ ] B. Are
   - [ ] C. Have

4. He usually _______ to work by car.
   - [ ] A. goes
   - [ ] B. is going
   - [ ] C. has gone

**Answers:** 1-C, 2-B, 3-C, 4-A

---

### Exercise 4: Correction Exercise
**Level: Advanced**

1. I am living in this city since 2015.
   - **Correction**: _______________________________

2. She has been study English for three years.
   - **Correction**: _______________________________

3. He work in a hospital right now.
   - **Correction**: _______________________________

4. I have went to London last year.
   - **Correction**: _______________________________

**Answers:**
1. I have been living in this city since 2015.
2. She has been studying English for three years.
3. He is working in a hospital right now. (or "He works in a hospital" if it's a permanent job, not "right now")
4. I went to London last year.

## Key Takeaways

✅ **Present Simple** = habits, facts, routines ("I study English.")

✅ **Present Continuous** = happening now, temporary, future arrangement ("I am studying English.")

✅ **Present Perfect** = past action connected to now, no exact time ("I have studied English.")

✅ **Present Perfect Continuous** = duration of an ongoing (or just-finished) action ("I have been studying English.")

## Practice Tips

📌 **Daily Practice**: Write one sentence about your day in each of the four present tenses.
📌 **Watch English**: Listen for "have/has" + past participle vs. "am/is/are" + -ing and identify which tense is used.
📌 **Keep a Journal**: Note moments you weren't sure which present tense to use, and review the rule afterward.
📌 **Review Rules**: If there's an exact finished time (yesterday, last year) → Past Simple, not Present Perfect.
📌 **Speak Out Loud**: Practice contrasting pairs like "I work here" vs. "I've worked here for 3 years" vs. "I'm working here this month."
